Your AI meter is running

An electricity meter shows you power you'd never otherwise notice using. This does the same for AI — it counts how much AI-generated content you take in, so an invisible habit becomes something you can actually look at.

What it watches

ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini conversations, plus Google's AI Overviews. Only the length of what AI produces is measured — never the text itself.

Where your data goes

Nowhere. It's stored in your browser and never transmitted. There's no server, no account, and no analytics — the extension makes no network requests at all.

What to do now

Nothing. Just use AI as you normally would and come back in a few days. A profile needs about a week of history before it means much.

Your data

Everything this extension records stays in your browser. It is never sent anywhere — there is no server, no account, and no analytics.

How this works, and what it can't tell you

The unit (aiU)

Roughly 500 characters of AI output is 1 aiU, multiplied by a category weight. Like a kilowatt-hour, it's a convenient stand-in rather than something meaningful in itself — useful for comparing one week against another, not as an absolute quantity.

The categories

  • Direct query (×1) — you asked, and used the answer.
  • Delegated creation (×1.2) — longer replies more likely to be content you reuse. Currently inferred from length alone, not from what you actually did with it.
  • Ambient (×0.3) — you never asked; it appeared anyway, like a search AI Overview. Weighted lowest because you didn't direct it.

The profile

Scored on how you use AI, not how much. Sending more messages does not raise it. It combines tool breadth (25%), the share of use that was deliberate rather than passive (45%), and consistency across days (30%). Someone whose intake is mostly ambient search summaries scores lower than someone making fewer, more deliberate queries. Below 5 active days or 25 events it's capped and marked provisional, because consistency is meaninglessly perfect when you've only just started.

Man-days

Assumes ~3,000 characters/hour for researched writing and an 8-hour day. "0.9 man-days" means this volume of text would have taken about 0.9 days to write from scratch — not that you saved 0.9 days, since that assumes you'd have written all of it yourself, at that length, unaided. The efficiency multiplier is just the ratio of reading speed to writing speed: a fact about reading, not about you.

What this can't tell you

Whether any of it was correct, whether you used it, whether you understood or checked it, or whether you could have done the work yourself. It measures consumption, and consumption isn't competence. It's a mirror for your own habits — not evidence about anyone, including you.
